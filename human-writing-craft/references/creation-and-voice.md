# Creation and voice calibration

Subtractive editing can remove clichés. It cannot create a voice on its own. Voice comes from material, judgment, selection, and recurring habits.

This guide handles new writing and voice matching without inventing a biography or turning a writer's highlights into a caricature.

## 1. Material comes before style

Before drafting substantial non-fiction, identify the available support units:

- firsthand experience supplied by the writer
- verified facts, dates, numbers, and quotations
- concrete examples or cases
- decisions and tradeoffs
- failures, surprises, constraints, and later results
- opposing evidence or a meaningful exception
- a reasoning step that connects two supported facts

A topic is not material. `AI changes writing` is a topic. A dated workflow change, an editor's observation, a study result, and a concrete before/after process are material.

### Section sufficiency test

Every major section should have a job and support:

| Section job | Acceptable support |
|---|---|
| explain | mechanism, definition, example, comparison |
| argue | evidence, reasoning, counterexample, limit |
| narrate | action, choice, conflict, consequence |
| instruct | prerequisite, ordered action, failure branch |
| evaluate | criterion, observation, comparison, tradeoff |

If a planned section has only a label such as “benefits,” “challenges,” or “future,” either find material or remove it.

### When material is thin

Use this order:

1. Research public facts when the task permits and current information is needed.
2. Ask for private experience only when the piece depends on it.
3. Narrow the claim or shorten the piece.
4. State uncertainty or the missing evidence.

Do not inflate length with fictional customers, generic scenarios, repeated explanations, or invented sensory detail.

## 2. Establish a speaking position

Internally answer:

1. Who is speaking, and what gives them standing?
2. What triggered this piece now?
3. What do they know directly, and what is inference?
4. What do they believe or prefer?
5. Where does that judgment stop?
6. What question will the reader naturally ask next?

A speaking position is not a role-play costume. `A tired engineer at 2 a.m.` is useless unless the source actually supplies that experience. A valid temporary position might be:

> An experienced front-end developer explaining a migration mistake to peers, using verified project details, direct language, and mild self-criticism.

## 3. Draft positively, not from a blacklist

For new prose:

- begin with a supported fact, scene, tension, decision, or claim
- let the next paragraph answer the question created by the previous one
- give each paragraph one dominant job
- make abstraction earn its place through nearby evidence
- state a position when the writer or material supports it
- allow normal repetition of key terms
- end when the thought is complete

Do not write a deliberately “AI” first draft and hope a later washing pass will create authorship. Prevention is cheaper than repair.

## 4. Build a voice profile from samples

Use the writer's own samples or an authorized brand guide. Five to ten representative samples are useful when available, but fewer can still help.

Prefer samples that are:

- written for a similar audience and purpose
- continuous prose rather than isolated quotes
- ordinary work, not only viral or award-winning pieces
- spread across time
- identified by the user as genuinely theirs
- minimally edited by other people or models, when known

Do not silently treat another writer's work as the user's personal voice. For public or living writers, abstract high-level traits instead of reproducing signature phrasing.

## 5. Extract the median, not the highlight reel

Separate observations into three bands.

### Baseline habits

These appear across most samples and should control most of the output:

- typical formality
- sentence and paragraph range
- usual order of claim and evidence
- common transition style
- normal certainty and qualification
- ordinary punctuation
- preferred technical vocabulary
- degree of first person and direct address

### Occasional signatures

These appear selectively:

- dry asides
- fragments
- parenthetical corrections
- unusual metaphors
- rhetorical questions
- profanity
- sharp single-sentence paragraphs

Use them only when the same function arises. Approximate their frequency; do not schedule them.

### Outliers

One-off experiments, unusually emotional passages, and memorable lines are not permanent rules. Do not promote them into every paragraph.

## 6. Distinguish voice from topic artifacts

A sample set may repeat words because every piece discusses the same product. Those terms are not necessarily voice traits.

Separate:

- **domain vocabulary:** required by subject
- **publication conventions:** required by platform
- **voice habits:** choices the writer makes across subjects
- **content facts:** never imitate outside their source context

## 7. Write a compact voice contract

A useful profile contains observable behavior, not adjectives alone.

Weak:

> Smart, warm, authentic, and engaging.

Useful:

> Opens with the practical consequence rather than background. Uses medium-length explanatory sentences, then a short correction. Repeats technical terms instead of cycling synonyms. Makes disagreement explicit but avoids sarcasm. Uses first person only for direct experience. Ends on a remaining constraint rather than a slogan.

## 8. Anti-overfitting rules

Do not:

- copy phrases from samples
- use every identified trait in every section
- make punctuation a quota
- convert occasional mistakes into style requirements
- exaggerate dialect, slang, or emotion
- infer private beliefs or biography
- imitate the most extreme sample instead of the median

A good voice match should feel unsurprising beside the samples, not like a parody of them.

## 9. Human taste gate

Objective checks can verify facts, links, versions, required sections, and explicit banned phrases. They cannot decide whether a passage “sounds like me.”

For important voice work, compare two versions anonymously when practical:

- A: current or baseline version
- B: revised version

Ask the writer which one feels more like their normal work and why. Record the specific preference, not just the winner. Update the profile only after the same preference appears across more than one piece.

Do not train toward a detector score or toward a validator model's taste. Human preference is the gate for voice.

## 10. Fiction and invented detail

Fiction authorizes invention. It does not authorize generic atmosphere.

Each scene should change at least one of:

- a character's goal
- available information
- relationship pressure
- physical position
- risk or consequence
- the reader's interpretation

Sensory detail should belong to the viewpoint and action. Avoid reusable atmosphere packs such as metallic blood, electric air, or a room that smells like ambition unless the story has earned that exact image.

## 11. Stop condition

A voice profile is useful when it predicts choices without dictating every sentence. Stop refining it when new rules merely restate earlier ones or when outputs become less varied than the samples.
